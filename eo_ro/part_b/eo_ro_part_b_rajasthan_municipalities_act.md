## 📖 CHAPTER I: PRELIMINARY (Sections 1-2)

#### Section 1: Short title, extent and commencement
1. **Title:** This Act is called the "Rajasthan Municipalities Act, 2009".
2. **Extent:** It applies to the **whole of Rajasthan, EXCLUDING Cantonment Areas**.
3. **Commencement:** It comes into force on the date the State Government notifies in the Official Gazette (which was 15 Sept 2009).

> **Deep Dive: Cantonment Areas** 
> Cantonment areas (like Nasirabad in Rajasthan) are military stations. They are administered directly by the Central Government (Ministry of Defence), not by the State Government. Hence, the State's Municipality Act does not apply there.

#### Section 2: Definitions
This section contains a long list of definitions used throughout the Act.
*   **Backward Classes:** Citizens other than SC/ST notified by State Govt.
*   **Bio-medical waste:** Waste from human/animal treatment, research, biological testing (includes anatomical waste, discarded medicines, sharps, etc.).
*   **Builder/Developer:** Agency/individual constructing a complex on their own or others' land.
*   **Building:** Any structure (foundation, walls, roof, balcony) excluding a tent, shamiana, or tarpaulin shelter. Boundary walls less than 3 meters high are also excluded from the definition of a "building".
*   **Complex:** A commercial building with **25 or more units**, or a residential building with **10 or more units**.
*   **Chairperson:** Chairman (Board), President (Council), Mayor (Corporation).
*   **Chief Municipal Officer (CMO):** Commissioner (Corporation & Council), Executive Officer (Board).
*   **Clear Days:** Includes Sundays and holidays, but EXCLUDES the day the notice is received and the actual day of the event specified in the notice.
*   **Dangerous Disease:** Cholera, plague, smallpox, diphtheria, tuberculosis, leprosy, influenza, encephalitis, poliomyelitis, syphilis, or any other notified disease.
*   **Fire Station:** Any post declared by the *State Government* to be a fire station.
*   **Latest Census Figures:** Figures from the last preceding published census.
*   **Nuisance:** Any act/thing causing injury, danger, annoyance to sight, smell, hearing, or dangerous to life/health.
*   **Public Place:** Space open to public use, whether it is owned by the Municipality or not.
*   **Public Securities:** Securities of the Rajasthan Govt, Central Govt, any State Govt, or specially authorized by State Govt.

> [!WARNING]
> **Possible Confusion:** Note the definition of a **"Complex"** strictly relies on the number of units. Commercial = 25+ units, Residential = 10+ units. This is a highly objective data point loved by examiners.

## 📖 CHAPTER II: CONSTITUTION AND GOVERNMENT OF MUNICIPALITIES (Sections 3-50)

#### Section 3: Delimitation of Municipalities
This section gives the **State Government** the supreme power to create, alter, or dissolve municipal boundaries.
1. The State Govt can:
   - Declare a new local area as a Municipality.
   - Include a new area into an existing Municipality.
   - Exclude an area from a Municipality.
   - Amalgamate (merge) or split Municipalities.
   - Declare that an area ceases to be a Municipality.
2. **Elections upon change:** If an area is newly added, elections for that new area must be held within **six months**. 
3. **Removal of members:** If an area is excluded, the members representing that area will be removed.
4. **Amalgamation:** If Municipality A merges into Municipality B, the members of A automatically become members of B until B's term ends.
5. **Boundary Marks:** It is the duty of the Municipality to erect and maintain boundary marks at its own cost, approved by the District Collector.
6. **Funds upon cessation:** If an area ceases to be a Municipality entirely, its funds and properties vest in the **State Government**. If it is merged into another local authority (like a Panchayat), the funds vest in that new authority.
7. **Transition from Village to Municipality:** When a village becomes a Municipality, it ceases to be a village. The Sarpanch, Up-Sarpanch, and Panchas automatically become the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, and members of the new Municipality until new elections are held.

> **Deep Dive: Delimitation & Cessation**
> *Delimitation* means drawing or redrawing the boundaries of a political region. *Cessation* means coming to an end (ceasing to exist).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight:** In the 2022 EO/RO Exam, a question was framed around the 6-month timeline for holding elections in newly added areas. Always remember this 6-month rule.

#### Section 4: Power to exempt Municipal Board from operation of Act provisions
1. The State Government can exempt any **Municipal Board** from specific provisions of this Act if those provisions are deemed "unsuited" for them.
2. This exemption must be notified in the Official Gazette with reasons recorded in writing.
3. The State Govt can make special alternative rules for that exempted Board.

#### Section 5: Establishment and incorporation of Municipality
1. **Transitional Area (Village to Town):** Municipal Board (Nagar Palika).
2. **Smaller Urban Area:** Municipal Council (Nagar Parishad).
3. **Larger Urban Area:** Municipal Corporation (Nagar Nigam).
4. Every Municipality is a "body corporate" (meaning it has a legal identity, can own property, and can sue or be sued in its own name) with a perpetual succession and a common seal.
5. **Exceptions (Industrial & Tourist Areas):**
   - The **Governor** can declare an area as an "Industrial Township", meaning no Municipality will be constituted there.
   - The **State Government** can exclude a historic/tourist area and hand it over to a Development Authority instead.

#### Section 6: Composition of Municipality
1. **Minimum Seats:** All seats are filled by direct election from Wards. The minimum number of seats in any Municipality is **13**.
2. **Additional Representatives:**
   - **MLA:** The local MLA represents the area in the Board, Council, and Corporation, and has a right to vote.
   - **MP (Lok Sabha):** The local MP represents the area in the Council and Corporation (NOT the Board), and has a right to vote.
   - **Nominated Members:** State Govt nominates experts in municipal administration:
     - **3 persons** in Municipal Board. *(Note: This was originally 4 in the 2009 Act, amended later to 6, then 8. As per latest amendments, it's 6 for Board, 8 for Council, 12 for Corp. Keep an eye on recent current affairs for exact numbers).* Let's stick to the base Act: 4 for Board, 5 for Council, 6 for Corp. 
     - Nominated members **do not have the right to vote** in meetings.
     - State Govt can withdraw nominated members at any time.

> **Deep Dive: What do nominated members actually do?**
> *(Note: This is not explicitly stated in the original Act document, but is provided here for your easy understanding).*
> Since they are experts (e.g., in urban planning, public health, law, or engineering), they serve in an **advisory capacity**. They participate in debates, offer technical insights, and help shape policies that elected politicians might not fully understand. They guide the municipality but cannot vote on the final decisions.

3. **Reservation System:**
   - **SC/ST:** Reserved based on their population proportion.
   - **Backward Classes (OBC):** Reserved such that the combined SC+ST+OBC seats do not cross 50%. However, OBC reservation cannot exceed 21%. (If SC+ST is less than 70%, at least 1 OBC seat is guaranteed).
   - **Women:** **50% of ALL seats** (including SC/ST/OBC categories) are reserved for women. *(Amended from 33% to 50%)*.
4. **Re-determination:** Seats are recalculated after every census, but it won't affect the existing Municipality until its 5-year term expires.

> [!WARNING]
> **Possible Confusion:** Remember the voting rights! Elected members, MLAs, and MPs **can vote**. Nominated members **CANNOT vote**. Also, MPs do not sit in the small Municipal Boards, only in Councils and Corporations.

#### Section 7: Term of office
1. The term of every Municipality is **5 years** from the date appointed for its **first meeting**.
2. If a Municipality is dissolved early, the newly elected Municipality will only serve for the **remainder of the original 5-year term**, not a fresh 5 years.

#### Section 8: Municipal government to vest in Municipality
The municipal government vests in the Municipal Board, Council, or Corporation through their **Chairpersons**, who are charged with carrying out the provisions of this Act.

> **Deep Dive: Vesting**
> "Vesting" means assigning the legal power, authority, and ownership to someone or some entity. The ultimate power belongs to the Municipality, exercised through its Chairperson.

#### Section 9: Division into wards
1. For elections, a Municipality is divided into wards.
2. The number of wards = The total number of seats fixed for the Municipality.
3. Wards are created based on population equality, meaning each ward should roughly represent the same number of people.

#### Section 10: Determination of wards
1. The **State Government** determines the extent of each ward and the allotment of reserved seats (SC, ST, OBC, Women).
2. Reserved seats are allotted by **rotation** to different wards.
3. **Geographical Rules for Wards:**
   - Wards must be geographically compact.
   - SC/ST reserved wards are placed where their population proportion is largest.
   - Numbering of wards (Ward 1, Ward 2, etc.) must strictly start from the **North-West corner** of the municipal area.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight:** The numbering starting from the "North-West corner" is a very classic question asked frequently in local self-government exams in Rajasthan.

#### Section 11: Election to the Municipality
1. The **State Election Commission (SEC)** is solely responsible for the superintendence, direction, and control of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections.
2. **Timelines for Elections:**
   - Must be completed *before* the expiry of the 5-year duration.
   - If dissolved early, new elections must be held within **six months** of dissolution.
   - **Exception:** If the remainder of the term of the dissolved Municipality is *less than 6 months*, it is NOT necessary to hold elections for that short period.

#### Section 12: Delegation of functions of State Election Commission
The SEC can delegate its powers and functions to a **Deputy Election Commissioner** or the **Secretary to the State Election Commission**.

#### Section 13: Electoral Roll for every ward
1. Prepared by the **Electoral Registration Officer** (appointed by SEC in consultation with State Govt).
2. Assistant Electoral Registration Officers can also be appointed.
3. **Conditions for Registration (Voter Eligibility):**
   - Must not be less than **18 years** of age on the **qualifying date** (which is the **1st day of January** of the year the roll is prepared).
   - Must be an **ordinarily resident** in that ward.
4. **Who is NOT an "ordinarily resident"?**
   - A patient in a mental hospital or a person in prison/legal custody.
   - A casual visitor in a hotel/hostel.
   - Merely owning a house does not make you an ordinarily resident.
5. **Exception for MPs/MLAs:** They do not lose their "ordinarily resident" status in their home ward just because they are absent due to their official duties in Parliament/Assembly.
6. A person can only be registered in **one ward** and only **once** *(meaning you cannot have your name in two different wards at the same time, nor twice in the same ward, to prevent double voting).*

> **Deep Dive: What happens if I get transferred? Can I change my ward and then change it back later?**
> **Yes.** Voter registration is tied to your **"ordinary residence"**. The rule "registered in one ward and only once" simply means you cannot be registered in two places *simultaneously*.
> *   If you are transferred and move to a new place, you become an ordinary resident of the new ward. You can apply to the Electoral Registration Officer to add your name to the new ward's roll. (Your name will then be deleted from your old ward's roll).
> *   When you get transferred back to your previous location and physically reside there again, you can repeat the process and have your name transferred back to your original ward.

#### Section 14: Disqualifications for registration in an electoral roll
You cannot be a registered voter if you:
1. Are not a citizen of India.
2. Are declared to be of unsound mind by a competent Court.
3. Are disqualified due to corrupt practices under any election law.
If someone incurs these disqualifications *after* being registered, their name will be struck off immediately.

#### Section 15: Making false declaration
If a person knowingly makes a false written statement regarding the preparation or revision of an electoral roll, they are punishable with:
- **Imprisonment** up to **1 year**, OR
- **Fine** between **₹2,000 to ₹5,000**, OR
- Both.

#### Section 16: Chief Electoral Officer
- An officer of the State Government, nominated by the SEC.
- Supervises the preparation of all electoral rolls and the conduct of all elections in the State under this Act.

#### Section 17: District Election Officers
- For each district, the SEC nominates a District Election Officer (must be a State Govt officer).
- The SEC can appoint more than one officer for a district if needed, specifying their separate areas of jurisdiction.
- They coordinate and supervise election work in the Municipalities of their district.

#### Section 18: Staff of local authorities etc. to be made available
Every local authority or State-financed institution MUST provide staff to the Electoral Registration Officer and Returning Officer whenever requested by the Chief Electoral Officer.

#### Section 19: Officers and staff deemed to be on deputation to the SEC
During election duty, all deployed staff are considered to be **on deputation to the State Election Commission** and are strictly under the SEC's control and superintendence.

#### Section 20: Breach of official duty in connection with electoral rolls
1. If any official breaches their duty without reasonable cause regarding electoral rolls, they face:
   - **Imprisonment:** **3 months to 2 years**, AND/OR
   - **Fine:** **₹1,000 to ₹2,000**.
2. **Protection:** No civil suit for damages can be filed against them for such acts.
3. **Cognizance:** A Court will only hear this case if the complaint is officially authorized by the SEC, Chief Electoral Officer, or District Election Officer.

> **Deep Dive: What does "Cognizance" mean here? Can a normal citizen file an FIR?**
> **No, a normal FIR won't lead to a trial in this specific case.** "Taking cognizance" means a judge officially accepting a case for trial. To protect election officers from false, politically motivated harassment by angry citizens, the law says the court **cannot** start a trial against an election officer for duty breaches unless top election authorities (SEC, CEO, or DEO) officially authorize or file the complaint themselves. You cannot bypass them and take the officer straight to court.

#### Section 21: Persons qualified for being members
To contest elections and become a member, a person must:
1. Be a registered elector in any ward of the Municipality.
2. Belong to the specific category (SC/ST/OBC/Women) if the seat is reserved.
3. Be at least **21 years** of age.
4. Have passed the **Secondary School Examination** (10th pass) from the Rajasthan Board or equivalent.
5. Have a **functional sanitary toilet** at their residence, and no family member (spouse, children, parents living with them) should defecate in the open.

#### Section 21A: Special qualification for election on certain seats
- The State Government can reserve certain seats strictly for candidates in the age group of **21 to 35 years**.
- Maximum of 2 seats each from SC, ST, OBC, or Women can be restricted this way.
- For unreserved seats, 1 out of every block of 5 unreserved seats is determined for this age group.

#### Section 22: Restriction on contesting elections for more than one ward
- A person **cannot** contest elections from more than one ward.
- If they file nominations for multiple wards, they must withdraw **all except one** by **3:00 PM** on the last date of withdrawal.
- **Consequence:** If they fail to withdraw, they are deemed to have withdrawn from **ALL** seats and cannot contest at all!

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight (Q84):** In the 2022 exam, candidates were asked where a person can contest from. Answer: "Only one ward."

#### Section 23: Restrictions on use of vehicles, loud-speakers etc.
- The SEC imposes restrictions on vehicles, loudspeakers, hoardings, cut-outs, and banners.
- **Penalty for violation:** Fine up to **₹2,000**.
- Furthermore, the person can be **disqualified** from being a member for up to **6 years**.

#### Section 24: General disqualifications for members
A person is disqualified from being a member (even if otherwise qualified) if they:
1. Are convicted for an offence involving moral turpitude with a sentence of **6 months or more**.
2. Are facing trial for a crime punishable by **5 years or more**, and the Court has framed charges.
3. Are convicted under the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954.
4. Are dismissed from Govt or Local Authority service for misconduct.
5. Are a debarred professional practitioner (like a suspended doctor or lawyer).
6. Hold a salaried job under Govt, university, or state-financed enterprise.
7. Are an undischarged insolvent or of unsound mind.
8. Have a direct/indirect financial interest in any municipal contract/work.
9. Are a paid legal practitioner fighting **against** the Municipality in Court.
10. Have **more than 2 children** (Exception: Those born before **27th November 1995** do not count towards disqualification, as long as the number doesn't increase).
11. Have arrears of municipal dues for more than **2 years** and recovery proceedings have started.

> [!WARNING]
> **Possible Confusion:** Merely having an ongoing criminal case doesn't disqualify you, UNLESS the potential punishment is 5+ years AND charges have been framed by the court. Similarly, the 2-child norm has a strict cut-off date: 27 Nov 1995.

#### Section 25: Right to vote
1. Only registered electors can vote. (Persons disqualified under Section 14 cannot vote).
2. You cannot vote in more than one ward. If you do, **all your votes are void**.
3. You cannot vote more than once in the same ward. If you do, **all your votes are void**.
4. **Prisoners:** Persons in prison or police custody CANNOT vote.
   - *Exception:* Persons under **preventive detention** CAN vote.

#### Section 26: Manner of voting at election
1. Voting is by ballot or **Voting Machines (EVMs)**.
2. No proxy voting is allowed.
3. Every elector has exactly one vote.

#### Section 27: Casual vacancy how to be filled
1. A casual vacancy (due to death, resignation, etc.) is filled via a **bye-election**.
2. The newly elected member only holds office for the **remainder of the original term**.
3. If the seat was reserved (SC/ST/OBC/Women), the bye-election must be contested by candidates of that same category.
4. If the remainder of the term is **less than 6 months**, the State Govt may choose to leave the seat unfilled until the next general election.

#### Section 28: Electoral offences
The electoral offences defined in the **Representation of the People Act, 1951** apply directly to these municipal elections.

> **Deep Dive: What is the Representation of the People Act, 1951?**
> This is a **Central Act** passed by the **Parliament of India** (not by a State Legislative Assembly). It is the primary law that governs elections for the national Parliament and all State Assemblies across the country. The Rajasthan Municipalities Act borrows the definitions of "electoral offences" directly from this central law.

#### Section 29: Corrupt practices
The following are corrupt practices that can void an election:
1. **Bribery:** Offering gifts/rewards to stand, withdraw, or vote.
2. **Undue Influence:** Threatening injury, social ostracism, or "divine displeasure" (spiritual curse).
3. **Appeals to Religion/Caste/National Symbols:** Asking for votes based on religion/caste, or using the national flag/emblem. (Official election symbols do not count as religious/national symbols).
4. **Enmity:** Promoting hatred between classes of citizens.
5. **Sati:** Propagating or glorifying the practice of Sati.
6. **False Statements:** Publishing false facts about a candidate's personal character.
7. **Free Conveyance:** Hiring vehicles to transport voters to the polling station (except voters using public transport at their own cost).
8. **Overspending:** Exceeding the authorized election expenditure limit.
9. **Govt Assistance:** Procuring election assistance from Govt employees (Gazetted officers, Armed forces, Police, Excise, Revenue officers like Patwaris).

#### Section 30: Jurisdiction of civil courts in electoral matters
1. Civil Courts have **NO jurisdiction** over ward delimitation, seat allotment, or electoral rolls.
2. An election can ONLY be challenged via an **election petition** presented under this Act.

#### Section 31: Election petition
1. An election petition must be filed within **1 month** from the date of election.
2. It is presented before the **District Judge** having territorial jurisdiction over the municipal area.
3. **Grounds for petition:**
   - The returned (winning) candidate was not qualified or was disqualified.
   - A corrupt practice was committed.
   - Improper rejection or acceptance of any nomination that materially affected the result.
   - Improper reception, refusal, or rejection of any vote.

> **Deep Dive: What exactly is an "Election Petition"?**
> An election petition is a formal legal complaint challenging the result of an election. If someone suspects that the winner cheated, was not actually qualified to run, or that votes were manipulated, they cannot just file a regular police case. They must file an "Election Petition" directly with the District Judge to officially demand that the election result be investigated and potentially cancelled.

#### Section 32: Appeals from orders of District Judge
1. An appeal against the District Judge's order lies to the **High Court**.
2. It must be filed within **30 days** from the date of the order (HC can extend if sufficient cause is shown).
3. The High Court will try to finally determine the appeal within **3 months**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight (Q86):** In 2022, they asked where an appeal from the District Judge's order under Section 31 lies. The answer is **High Court**. Note that they put 'District Judge' as a trick option!

#### Section 33: Procedure where election of all candidates is set aside
If the election of ALL the members, or **more than 2/3rd** of the total members of a Municipality, is declared void, the State Government shall **dissolve** the Municipality.

#### Section 34: Finality of orders and decision
The decision of the High Court is final and conclusive.

#### Section 35: Disqualifications
If a person is found guilty of an electoral offence (Sec 28) or a corrupt practice (Sec 29), they are disqualified from being a member for **6 years** from the date of conviction or the Judge's finding.

#### Section 36: Removal or reduction of period of disqualification
The **State Election Commission** has the power to remove the disqualification or reduce its period, for reasons recorded in writing.

#### Section 37: Oath of office
1. Every Chairperson and member must take an oath before an officer authorized by the State Government.
2. **Timeline:** Within **1 month** from the date of the first meeting of the Municipality (or from date of election/nomination if bye-election).
3. If they fail to take the oath within 1 month, they are deemed to have vacated their seat.
   - *Exception:* Time spent in jail as an undertrial or political prisoner doesn't count towards the 1-month limit.

#### Section 38: Resignation
1. A member can resign by giving written notice to the **Chairperson**.
2. The resignation must be attested by an **Executive Magistrate**.
3. It takes effect after **15 days** from the date of notice OR from the date the Chairperson accepts it, **whichever is earlier**.

#### Section 39: Removal of member
1. The **State Government** can remove a member on the following grounds:
   - Absent for more than **3 consecutive general meetings** without leave.
   - Failure to take the oath (Sec 37).
   - Incurs a disqualification (Sec 14 or 24).
   - Deliberate neglect of duties, misconduct, or disgraceful conduct.
2. **Procedure:** The State Govt appoints a **Judicial Officer** (of the rank of a District Judge) to conduct an inquiry. The member is given a chance to explain.
3. The State Govt can **suspend** the member pending the inquiry.
4. The final order of removal is published in the Official Gazette and cannot be questioned in any Court.

#### Section 40: Inquiry into certain allegations after expiry of term of office
An inquiry for misconduct or disgraceful conduct can be initiated or continued even **after** the member's term has expired.

#### Section 41: Disability of members removed under Section 39
A member removed for misconduct/disgraceful conduct, or against whom adverse findings are recorded after their term, is **ineligible for re-election for a period of 6 years**.

#### Section 42: Restriction on simultaneous holding of office
1. A person cannot simultaneously be a member of a Municipality AND a member of Parliament (MP), State Legislative Assembly (MLA), or a Panchayati Raj Institution.
2. If an MP/MLA/Panchayat member is elected to a Municipality, they must resign from their former seat within **14 days**.
3. If they fail to resign from their former seat within 14 days, they automatically **cease to be a member of the Municipality**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Possible Confusion:** They do NOT lose their MP/MLA seat. They lose their *Municipal* seat if they hold onto both for 14 days. 

#### Section 43: Every Municipality to have a Chairperson and a Vice-Chairperson
1. **Corporation:** Mayor & Deputy Mayor.
   **Council:** President & Vice-President.
   **Board:** Chairman & Vice-Chairman.
2. **Reservation:** Offices are reserved for SC, ST, OBC, and Women. State Govt allocates these across the state.
3. **Leave of absence:** A Chairperson/Vice-Chairperson cannot be absent for more than 1 month without leave. Maximum leave granted cannot exceed **6 months**.
4. **No-Confidence:** They vacate office immediately if a no-confidence motion is passed against them.
5. **Resignation:** Vice-Chairperson gives resignation to the Chairperson. Chairperson gives it to an officer authorized by State Govt. Takes effect after **30 days** or upon acceptance, whichever is earlier.
6. **Vacancy filling:** A casual vacancy for Chairperson must be filled within **6 months** (State Govt can extend by max 3 months).

#### Section 44: Determination of validity of election of Chairperson or Vice-Chairperson
1. Challenged via an election petition to the **District Judge**.
2. Must be presented by a defeated candidate or rejected nominee.
3. Requires a deposit of **₹1,000**.
4. If the Judge finds the petition frivolous, the ₹1,000 deposit is forfeited to the State Government.

#### Section 45: Core municipal functions
It is the mandatory duty of the Municipality to provide for:
- Public health, sanitation, solid waste management, drainage.
- Lighting public streets.
- Extinguishing fires.
- Securing dangerous buildings.
- Disposing of the dead (cremation/burial grounds).
- Registering births and deaths.
- Promoting population control, family welfare (added later).
- Slum improvement, poverty alleviation.
- Construction of roads, bridges, subways.
- Preventing cruelty to animals.

#### Section 46: Other municipal functions
Subject to financial capability, the Municipality may also undertake:
- **Environment:** Social forestry, flower shows, nurseries.
- **Public Health:** Mass inoculations, maintaining public tanks.
- **Education/Culture:** Libraries, museums, statues, civic education.
- **Public Welfare:** Shelters for destitute, hospitals, rescue homes.
- **Community Relations:** Fairs, exhibitions, civic receptions.

#### Section 47: Functions assigned by the Government
The State Government can mandate the Municipality to perform any other additional functions.

#### Section 48: Functions of Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson
1. **Chairperson's duties:** Convene regular meetings, preside over them, and watch over the financial and executive administration.
2. **Note of Dissent (Chairperson):** If a resolution is illegal or against municipal interests, the Chairperson must record their opinion and refer it to the **State Government**. The State Govt's decision is final.
3. **Vice-Chairperson:** Exercises delegated powers and acts as Chairperson in their absence.

#### Section 49: Powers and duties of the Chief Municipal Officer (CMO)
1. **Records:** Responsible for custody and maintenance of all municipal records.
2. **Note of Dissent (CMO):** If a resolution or Chairperson's order is illegal, the CMO MUST tender advice mentioning the law, record this advice, put up a note of dissent, and inform the State Govt within **7 days**.
3. **Personal Liability:** If the CMO neglects to do this, they are **personally liable** for any financial loss the Municipality suffers as a result!
4. **Authentication:** The CMO signs/authenticates all licenses and permissions. All correspondence is ordinarily in the name of the CMO.

#### Section 50: Handing over charge
1. When a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson, or member vacates office (resignation, suspension, dissolution), they must hand over all papers and properties.
2. **Chain of handover:**
   - Chairperson hands over to Vice-Chairperson.
   - If the Chairperson seat was reserved (SC/ST/OBC/Women), the charge goes to a Vice-Chairperson of the SAME category. If none, then to a member of the same category.
   - Vice-Chairperson hands over to Chairperson.
3. The person receiving the charge can hold it for a maximum of **60 days**, or until the new person takes over, whichever is earlier.
4. If a person refuses to hand over charge, the authorized person simply assumes charge legally.

---

### CHAPTER III: Conduct of Business and Ward Committee (Sections 51 - 66)

#### Section 51: Provisions in regard to meetings of a Municipality
1. **Ordinary Meeting:** Must be held once within **60 days**, and a minimum of **6 meetings** must be held in a calendar year.
2. **Special Meeting:** 
   - The Chairperson must call a special meeting within **7 days** of receiving a written request signed by at least **1/3rd** of the elected members.
   - If the Chairperson fails to call it, the **Chief Municipal Officer (CMO)** must call it within **10 days** after those 7 days expire.

#### Section 52: Rights and privileges of individual members
Every member has the right to:
1. Call attention to neglect of municipal work, wastage of property, or civic problems.
2. Put questions to the Chairperson and move resolutions.
3. **Inspect municipal records** without fee, after giving due notice to the CMO.

#### Section 53: Motion of no-confidence against Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson
1. **Time Restriction:** A no-confidence motion CANNOT be brought within **2 years** of them assuming office.
   - *Exception:* If elected in a bye-election, the motion cannot be brought unless **half of their tenure** has expired.
2. **Subsequent Motions:** If a no-confidence motion fails, another one cannot be brought for the next **2 years**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight:** The 2-year cooling-off period for no-confidence motions is a frequent examination topic. 

#### Section 54: Constitution of Wards Committee
1. Constituted in Municipalities having a population of **3 Lakhs or more**.
2. Can consist of one or more wards.
3. **Composition:**
   - The elected members representing the wards in the committee.
   - Maximum **5 nominated members** (must be >25 years old, have special knowledge in municipal admin, and not be disqualified).
4. **Chairman:** 
   - If the committee is for a single ward, that ward's member is the Chairman.
   - If multiple wards, the members elect a Chairman from amongst themselves annually.
5. **Quorum:** **3 members** (including the Chairman).
6. **Duration:** Co-extensive with the duration of the Municipality.

#### Section 55: Committees
1. **Executive Committee:** The most important committee. Consists of:
   - Chairperson & Vice-Chairperson.
   - Leader of the Opposition.
   - 7 elected members (incl. 2 women) for Corporation/Council OR max 5 elected members for a Board.
   - Chairpersons of all other committees.
   - The CMO is the *ex-officio* Secretary.
2. **Other Committees** (Maximum 10 members each):
   - Finance
   - Health and Sanitation (1 committee for <=50 wards, 2 for 51-75, 3 for >75)
   - Lighting of public streets (same ward ratio as Health)
   - Building permission and works
   - Slum improvement
   - Women & Children Development, Poverty Alleviation
   - Rules and Bye-laws
   - Compounding of offences
   - *Additional Committees:* Max 8 (Corporation), Max 6 (Council), Max 4 (Board).
3. **Timeline:** Must be constituted within **90 days** of the Municipality's constitution, otherwise the State Govt will constitute them.

#### Section 56: When persons other than members may serve on committee
1. Non-members with special qualifications can be appointed to any committee **EXCEPT the Executive Committee**.
2. Requires a resolution supported by at least **1/2 of the total members**.
3. Non-members cannot exceed **1/3rd** of the total members of that specific committee.

#### Section 57: Chairman of committee
- If the Chairperson of the Municipality is a member of any committee, they are the **ex-officio Chairman**.
- If the Vice-Chairperson is a member (and Chairperson is not), they are the ex-officio Chairman.
- Otherwise, the Municipality appoints one, or the committee elects its own.

#### Section 58: Procedure at meetings
- Ordinary meetings held within **2 months**.
- Special meetings can be called by the Chairman within **2 days** upon written request by the Chairperson or 2 committee members.
- **Quorum:** **One-half (1/2)** of the members.

#### Section 59: Subordination of committees
Committees must follow instructions of the Municipality. A committee cannot approve any expenditure unless it is already approved in the municipal budget.

#### Section 60: General functions of the Ward Committee
Functions include solid waste management, sanitation supervision, encouraging harmony, mobilizing voluntary labour, maintaining parks and street lights, and assisting in tax collection.

#### Section 61: Delegation of powers
Powers can be delegated to committees. If the Chairperson's office is vacant and there is no Vice-Chairperson, powers are delegated to a member (if it was a reserved seat, it must be delegated to a member of the same reserved category).

#### Section 62: Acts not vitiated by disqualifications
Any defect in the election or appointment of a member/Chairperson does NOT invalidate the acts or proceedings of the Municipality or committee that were done before they were declared disqualified.

#### Section 63: Liability for costs and expenses
- The Municipal Fund is generally liable for costs/contracts. Chairperson/members are **NOT personally liable**.
- **BUT**, they ARE personally liable for any loss, waste, or misapplication of municipal money/property if it is a direct consequence of their **misconduct or neglect**.

#### Section 64: Official not to be interested in contract
Municipal officers/employees cannot have a direct or indirect share/interest in any municipal contract.

#### Section 65: Penalty for member/officer interested in a contract
- **Member:** Fine up to **₹5,000**.
- **Officer/Servant:** Fine up to **₹5,000** AND disciplinary action.

#### Section 66: Members etc. to be deemed public servant
Every member, officer, and employee of the Municipality is deemed a "public servant" under **Section 21 of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)**.

### CHAPTER IV: Municipal Property (Sections 67 - 75)

#### Section 67: Power to acquire and hold property
The Municipality can acquire (by gift, purchase, etc.) and hold movable/immovable property both **within and outside** its municipal limits.

#### Section 68: Vesting of property
1. Properties that vest in the Municipality: public lands, city walls, gates, markets, slaughterhouses, public tanks, drains, streets, parks, lamps, solid waste, and stray animals.
2. The State Govt can **resume** any Government land vested in the Municipality if it is mismanaged or required in public interest.

#### Section 69: Acquisition of property by agreement, exchange, lease, grant
The Municipality requires the **prior approval of the State Government** to acquire property by agreement, exchange, or lease.

#### Section 69A: Surrender of rights and issue of lease deed
A person holding non-agricultural land without a lease can surrender their rights to the Municipality to obtain formal leasehold rights (upon paying determined fees).

#### Section 70: Compulsory acquisition of land
If the Municipality requires land, the State Government will acquire it on their behalf using the **Land Acquisition Act, 1894**. The Municipality must pay the compensation and charges.

#### Section 71: Allotment/regularization of certain lands
Lands placed at the disposal of the Municipality under the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956 can be allotted/regularized on payment of urban assessment/premium.

#### Section 72: Contracts by officers appointed by Government
An officer appointed by the State Govt to execute municipal works can make contracts for that work, and the Municipality must pay for it.

#### Section 73: Provisions relating to transfers of property and contracts
1. Any transfer of Govt land by the Municipality must be confirmed by the prescribed authority.
2. **EWS / LIG Quota:** If a Municipality transfers land for group housing or township projects, at least **20%** of plots/units MUST be allotted to Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) and Low Income Groups (LIG) at concessional rates.
3. The State Govt can cancel or suspend any illegal transfer of land. Unauthorized occupants will face eviction under the Rajasthan Public Premises (Eviction of Unauthorized Occupants) Act, 1964.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight (Q93):** In the 2022 exam, candidates were asked the minimum percentage of plots/units to be allotted to EWS and low-income groups in housing projects on municipal land. Answer: **20%**.

#### Section 74: Inventory and map of immovable municipal property
1. The Municipality must maintain an inventory and map of all its immovable properties.
2. Copies must be deposited with the **Director of Local Bodies** within 1 year.
3. The CMO prepares an annual statement indicating any changes.

#### Section 75: Maintenance of records of urban land
The Municipality must prepare and regularly update records of urban land and private properties within the municipal area in consultation with other departments.

### CHAPTER V: Municipal Finance and Municipal Fund (Sections 76 - 89A)

#### Section 76: State Finance Commission (SFC)
1. The SFC reviews the financial position of the Municipalities.
2. It makes recommendations to the **Governor** regarding:
   - The distribution of net proceeds of taxes, duties, tolls, and fees between the State and Municipalities.
   - The assignment of taxes/fees to Municipalities.
   - Grants-in-aid from the Consolidated Fund of the State.
   - Measures needed to improve municipal finances.
3. The Governor lays these recommendations before the State Legislature.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight (Q104):** In 2022, they asked who the State Finance Commission makes its recommendations to. The answer is the **Governor**.

#### Section 77 & 78: Implementation and Financial Assistance
- The State Government determines the devolution, assignment of taxes, and grants based on the SFC recommendations.
- The State Govt can give grants to the Municipality for its annual development plan, with or without conditions.

#### Section 79: Municipal Fund
1. All moneys realized under this Act are credited to the **Municipal Fund**, held in trust by the Municipality.
2. Receipts and expenditures are kept under specific heads of accounts (e.g., solid waste, drainage, commercial projects).
3. Every head of account is split into a **revenue account** and a **capital account**.

#### Section 80 & 81: Application of Municipal Fund
- Moneys can only be spent for purposes of this Act.
- **General Rule:** No payment can be made unless it is covered by a **current budget grant** and there is a sufficient balance available.
- **Exceptions:** Payments can be made WITHOUT a budget grant for:
   - Refunds of taxes or repayment of contractor deposits.
   - Emergency works required by the State Govt.
   - Outbreak of dangerous diseases, natural hazards, or emergencies.
   - Sums payable under a court decree or compromise.
   - Salaries and wages.
   - Taking immediate action to avert a sudden threat to life or property.

#### Section 82, 83 & 84: Special Expenditures
- **Section 82:** State Govt can require the CMO to execute urgent works in the public interest, paying from the Municipal Fund. The State Govt later reimburses the cost.
- **Section 83:** Expenditure outside municipal limits requires the **approval of the State Government**.
- **Section 84:** State Govt can earmark a portion of the Municipal Fund to be utilized *exclusively* for a specific purpose.

#### Section 85 & 86: Operation of Accounts and Surplus Moneys
1. Surplus money in one account can be transferred to another head of account.
   - *Exception:* Surplus money in the **Commercial Projects Account** CANNOT be transferred to the General Account.
2. Un-transferred surplus can be invested in public securities, small savings schemes, or scheduled banks.

#### Section 87: Preparation of Budget Estimate
1. The **Chief Municipal Officer (CMO)** prepares the budget estimate.
2. It must be prepared before **15th January** each year for the ensuing financial year.
3. The Chairperson must present it to the Municipality no later than **31st January**.
4. **Prior State Govt Approval Required:** The following proposals CANNOT be included in the budget without prior approval from the State Govt:
   - Creation of new posts or filling vacant posts.
   - Purchase of new vehicles.
   - Purchase of immovable property.
   - Grants-in-aid to private institutions/persons.
5. The budget must state the rates for taxes/fees and the loans to be raised.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight (Q95):** The 2022 exam asked for the deadline by which the CMO must prepare the budget estimate. Answer: Before **15th January**.

#### Section 88: Sanction of Budget Estimate
1. The Municipality must pass the budget by **15th February**.
2. A copy is sent to the State Govt, which has the power to direct binding changes.
3. **If the Municipality fails to pass it:** The CMO must prepare and submit the budget to the State Govt by **28th February**. The State Govt will approve it, and it will be deemed passed.
4. **Strict Liability:** Any work order issued without a proper budget provision makes the Chairperson and CMO jointly and severally liable to recover that expenditure!

#### Section 89: Power to alter budget grant
During the year, the Municipality can increase, reduce, or transfer budget grants, BUT it must have the **recommendation of the Finance Committee**.

#### Section 89A: Basic Services to the Urban Poor Fund
1. Every Municipality must constitute this separate fund for slum inhabitants and the urban poor.
2. **Minimum 25%** of the yearly budget grants must be earmarked for this fund.
3. It covers water supply, drainage, community toilets, affordable housing, health centers, etc. (It does NOT cover salaries/establishment expenses).
4. Unutilized funds **do NOT lapse**; they carry over to the next year.
5. Kept in a separate account in a nationalized bank.

### CHAPTER VI: Accounts and Audit (Sections 90 - 100)

#### Section 90, 91 & 92: Accounts and Financial Statements
1. The CMO prepares and maintains accounts.
2. The State Govt prepares a **"Municipal Accounting Manual"** detailing financial procedures.
3. Within **3 months** of the close of the financial year, the CMO must prepare a financial statement (income/expenditure) and a balance sheet (assets/liabilities).

#### Section 93 & 94: Audit of Accounts
1. The financial statement and balance sheet are first placed before the **Finance Committee**, which adopts them and remits them to the Auditor.
2. Auditing is done by the **Auditors of Local Fund Audit** (under the Rajasthan Local Fund Audit Act, 1954).
3. The Finance Committee considers the Auditor's report. If there is illegal payment or loss due to negligence, the Committee can **surcharge** the responsible person.
4. Appeals against a surcharge order go to the **State Government** (whose decision is final).

#### Section 95, 96 & 97: Submission and Action on Audit
1. The CMO places the audited accounts, Auditor's report, and defect replies before the Finance Committee, and then before the Municipality.
2. Adopted reports are forwarded to the **State Government**.
3. If there is a difference of opinion between the Auditor and the Municipality, the State Govt's decision is final and binding.

#### Section 98, 99 & 99A: Special, Internal, and CAG Audit
1. **Section 98 (Special Audit):** The State Govt or Municipality can appoint an auditor for a special audit of specific items.
2. **Section 99 (Internal Audit):** Can be provided for day-to-day accounts.
3. **Section 99A (CAG Audit):** The accounts shall also be audited by the **Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG)**. The CAG provides technical guidance, and the audit report is laid before the State Legislature.

#### Section 100: Annual Administration Report
1. The CMO prepares an annual report on the municipal administration for the preceding year.
2. After consideration by the Municipality, it must be submitted to the State Government.
3. **Timeline:** Must be submitted after **1st April** and no later than **30th June** every year.

### CHAPTER VII: Municipal Revenue (Sections 101 - 140)

#### Section 101: Internal revenues of Municipality
The internal revenues consist of:
1. Taxes levied by the Municipality.
2. User charges levied for civic services.
3. Fees and fines levied for regulatory/statutory functions.

#### Section 102: Obligatory taxes
The Municipality **MUST** levy these taxes if required by the State Government:
1. Tax on lands and buildings.
2. Tax on professions, trades, callings, and employment.
3. Toll on roads, bridges, and ferries.
4. Pollution control tax on trade/industries causing environmental pollution.
5. Tax on permissible display or advertisements on public places/private lands.
6. Any other tax the State Govt has the power to impose.
*Note: The State Govt can exempt a Municipality from levying these upon a justified request.*

#### Section 103: Other taxes that may be imposed
The Municipality **MAY** levy these taxes (subject to State Govt orders):
1. Tax on vehicles plying within the Municipality.
2. Tax on boats moored within the Municipality.
3. Lighting tax & Fire tax.
4. Tax on congregations, pilgrims, and tourists.
5. Tax on land/building used for hoardings.
6. Tax on deficit in parking spaces.
7. **Surcharge on stamp duty** (maximum **10%** of the stamp duty).

#### Section 104 & 105: User charges, Fees, and Fines
- **User charges:** Can be levied for drainage, solid waste management, parking, and stacking construction materials on public streets.
- **Fees & Fines:** Levied for sanctioning building plans, municipal licenses (plumbers, hawking, private markets, hospitals, hotels, slaughterhouses), and birth/death certificates.

#### Section 106: Development charge
- Can be levied on residential buildings >14 meters high, non-residential buildings, or for redevelopment.
- Must be paid in **advance** by the developer.
- It must be kept in a **separate account** and cannot be diverted for any other use.

#### Section 107: Exemption from taxation
1. Property belonging to the Municipality itself is exempt.
2. Property belonging to the Central/State Government is exempt from land/building, vehicle, and boat taxes (unless used for profit).
3. Places of **public worship** are strictly exempt from land/building taxes.
4. The State Govt can grant special exemptions via the Official Gazette.

#### Section 108, 109 & 110: Procedure for imposing or altering taxes
1. **Preliminary Procedure (Sec 108):** The Municipality selects the tax, prepares draft rules (specifying class, rate, valuation), and publishes them with a notice in the form set out in the **First Schedule**. Inhabitants get **1 month** to submit objections.
2. **Imposition (Sec 109):** After considering objections and passing a special resolution, it is notified in the Gazette. It can only come into force on **1st April, 1st July, 1st October, or 1st January**.
3. **Alteration/Abolition (Sec 110):** The exact same procedure is used to alter or abolish a tax.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight:** In a 2022 match-the-following question, the **First Schedule** was correctly matched with Section 108(b) (notice for preliminary procedure to impose a tax).

#### Section 111 & 112: Power to suspend or prohibit tax
- **State Govt (Sec 111):** Can suspend or abolish a tax if it is unfair in incidence or against the general public interest, and the Municipality fails to fix it.
- **Municipality (Sec 112):** Can suspend or abolish a tax imposed under Section 103 if it is in the public interest.

#### Section 113 & 114: Assessment of tax
- The State Govt appoints **Assessors**.
- The owner/occupier MUST furnish returns/info when requested. If they fail, the Assessor will assess the tax to the **best of his judgment**.

#### Section 115: Obligation to supply info for amendment (Penalty for failure)
1. If a building is newly built or enlarged, the owner must give notice within **1 month** of completion or occupation (whichever is earlier).
2. **Penalty:** If they fail to give notice, the Municipality can impose a penalty of up to **₹500** OR **10 times the amount of tax** payable on the enlargement for 3 months, **whichever is greater**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight (Q83):** The 2022 exam directly asked for the exact penalty under Section 115(1). Answer: ₹500 or 10 times the tax for 3 months, whichever is greater.

#### Section 116, 117, 118 & 119: Notice of transfer of title
1. When a property is sold, transferred, or the owner dies, notice MUST be given to the Municipality.
2. **Form of Notice (Sec 117):** Must be in the form of the **Second Schedule** or **Third Schedule**.
3. The transferee's name is then substituted in the municipal register.
4. **Failure to notify (Sec 119):** The original owner continues to be liable for taxes until the notice is given.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PYQ Highlight:** In a 2022 match-the-following question, the **Second Schedule** was correctly matched with Section 117.

#### Section 120: Tax from whom primarily leviable
- Primarily leviable from the actual occupier if they are the owner or a government leaseholder.
- If the property is let (rented), from the **lessor** (landlord).
- If sub-let, from the superior lessor.
- If not let, from the person holding the right to let it.

#### Section 121, 122 & 123: Appeals relating to taxation
1. Appeals against assessment or a notice of demand go to the **Commissioner** (Corporation) or **Regional Deputy Director of Local Bodies** (Council/Board).
2. Must be filed within **30 days**.
3. **Mandatory Condition:** **25%** of the claimed tax amount MUST be deposited with the Municipality before the appeal can be heard.

#### Section 124, 125 & 126: Jurisdiction, Savings, and Disclosures
- **Section 124:** Civil and criminal courts have NO jurisdiction over valuation, assessment, or taxation liability under this Act. The appellate authority's order is final.
- **Section 125 (Savings):** A clerical error or mistake in names does NOT invalidate a tax or notice.
- **Section 126:** If an inhabitant refuses to disclose info or furnishes untrue info, they face a fine of up to **₹1,000**.
